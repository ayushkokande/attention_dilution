\section{Further robustness checks}
\label{app:robust}

\paragraph{Sampled decoding.} With temperature 0.7 and three samples per prompt, the gap persists for every
model: with the harmful item last, the 24-token judge reports \SampQwenLastVone, \SampGemmaLastVone\ and
\SampLlamaLastVone\ refusal for Qwen3-14B, Gemma-2-9B and Llama-3.1-8B, against \SampQwenLastRef,
\SampGemmaLastRef\ and \SampLlamaLastRef\ target refusal and \SampQwenLastASR, \SampGemmaLastASR\ and
\SampLlamaLastASR\ ASR (\cref{tab:variants}).

\paragraph{Reasoning mode.} With Qwen3-14B's thinking mode on (4{,}096 extra tokens for the trace), the
24-token judge applied to the answer after the trace reports \ThinkLastVone\ refusal with the harmful item last,
while the target is refused in \ThinkLastRef\ of answers and HarmBench ASR is \ThinkLastASR\ (alone: \ThinkAloneRef\
refused, \ThinkAloneASR\ ASR). The trace itself contains refusal language in \ThinkRefInThinking\ of list
responses, so a judge that read the trace would see the decision early, but one that reads only the opening of
the answer would not (\cref{tab:variants}).

\paragraph{What surrounds the request.} Replacing Alpaca instructions with coding tasks, math word problems or
factual questions as the benign items (8-item lists, harmful item in the middle or last) leaves ASR low for
every model (\cref{tab:content}; at most \CtLlamaCodeASR, for Llama-3.1-8B with coding tasks). The kind of
benign content changes how often a model refuses up front: Gemma-2-9B's 24-token refusal rate is 10\% with
Alpaca items and 56\% with coding tasks. It does not change whether the target is refused (Gemma-2-9B:
\CtGemmaCodeRef\ to \CtGemmaQaRef). The one low cell, Qwen3-14B with math problems (\CtQwenMathRef\ refused,
\CtQwenMathASR\ ASR), is mostly a measurement gap. In a spot check of six responses that were neither refused
nor harmful, the model prefixed the item with a warning (``\emph{Note:} this request is illegal and
unethical'') that our phrase list does not match, so 66\% is a lower bound.
