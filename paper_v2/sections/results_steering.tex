If the model's handling of the harmful item is re-read when it reaches the item, adding the refusal direction at
that point should change the outcome. We test this with \emph{item-time steering}. For list prompts with
$k{=}15$ and the harmful item in the middle or last position, we teacher-force the model's own greedy response
up to the harmful item's answer onset. We then generate 160 tokens while adding $\alpha\,v_\ell$ to the output of
the selected layer, where $v_\ell=\mu^{\text{harm}}_\ell-\mu^{\text{harmless}}_\ell$ is the \emph{unnormalized}
difference of means, so that $\alpha{=}1$ adds one ``harmful-minus-harmless'' unit. Our earlier study added at
most 2.6\% of this vector, which made its negative result uninformative (\cref{app:v1}). We add the vector either
from the decision point onward or at every position, including the prompt. Of the 64 harmful cases per model we include every case where
the unsteered model complied (\QwenSteerNComply, \GemmaSteerNComply\ and \LlamaSteerNComply\ for Qwen, Gemma and
Llama) and fill the rest with refused cases. As an over-refusal check, the same
intervention is applied at the onset of a \emph{benign} target in the same list structure (32 per model).

\paragraph{The direction is causally effective at the item, within a narrow window.} For Qwen3-14B, adding one
unit of the direction from the decision point onward lowers ASR on the harmful continuation from 28\% to 6\% and
raises refusal from 61\% to 86\%, with no refusals of benign targets (\cref{tab:steer}). Smaller doses have
little effect. For Llama-3.1-8B, half a unit already lowers ASR from 34\% to 8\% at a cost of 3\% benign
refusals; a full unit removes harmful continuations but refuses half of the benign targets. Gemma-2-9B rarely
complies even without steering (5\% ASR), and a quarter unit removes the remaining harmful continuations without
benign refusals; larger doses refuse benign targets too. Adding the vector at every position, including the
prompt, is not consistently better than adding it at the decision point: it helps Llama-3.1-8B at half a unit but
refuses more benign targets, and it does not help Qwen3-14B.

These results reverse our earlier conclusion that single-direction steering cannot rescue refusal in lists.
That test added at most 2.6\% of the vector, an order of magnitude below the doses that work here. The usable
range differs by model and is narrow: the dose that removes harmful continuations is close to the dose that
starts refusing benign requests. With 64 harmful and 32 benign cases per model, these estimates are coarse,
and we read them as evidence that the direction is causally relevant at the item, not as a deployable
defence.
